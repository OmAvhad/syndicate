What’s broken today

Leaders get copied for free

A good trader’s wallet is public. Tools watch it and copy every move, so they can’t earn from their skill.

Bots trade ahead

Big followings mean big predictable buys. Bots jump in first, and followers pay higher prices.

Leaderboards are faked

One person runs many wallets, shows only the winners, and looks like a genius.

The fix, one idea at a time

Sealed signals. Each signal is encrypted with a room key that only followers hold (or per-message hybrid keys), then stored on Arkiv for 10 minutes. Anyone can download it; only active, paying followers can open it.

Signed by the trader. Priya signs every signal with her own profile key, so followers know it’s really her, even if our server is compromised.

Tags that change every time. Signals aren’t filed under a fixed room name. Each gets a new tag that only followers can compute, so outsiders can’t even tell which signals belong to the same room.

One shared poster. Our server posts every room’s signals from one wallet. It only ever sees ciphertext, so it can’t read or forge anything. If it goes down, Priya’s app can post directly.

One human, one seat. World ID proves each trader and follower is a unique real person, so nobody can fake a record with fifty wallets.

Untraceable trading wallets. Priya’s money sits in a private pool (Railgun, through the EF’s Kohaku SDK). Each trade uses a fresh single-use wallet that nobody can trace to the leader.

Followers protected by default. Copies are spread out: a random short delay, then each order split into smaller pieces over a few minutes. That blurs the burst of buys that would reveal a signal and gives bots less to jump ahead of.

Private mode for followers, opt-in. Followers who want it keep their money in the same private pool and copy from fresh burners, just like the leader. Best for larger amounts, since relayer fees are fixed.

Real execution, built-in fees. Trades run through Definitive Flash, with a small fee on every copied trade split between the leader and the platform.

A record nobody can edit. Before each trade, Priya posts a sealed, numbered promise. After it closes, she opens it and anyone can check her real result.

One trade, start to finish

  1. A few days before

    Priya funds a private balance

    EF KohakuShields her USDC into the Railgun pool, turning it into private notes only she can spend.

    She withdraws 10,000 USDC from her exchange to a brand-new wallet 0xA1A1…F001 and deposits it into the private pool. She never uses that wallet again.

    Public sees: a new wallet deposited into the pool, like thousands of others.

    Questions at this step
    Can we hide that Priya uses the private pool at all?

    Not fully: the first deposit is always public. But it comes from a fresh wallet funded straight from her exchange, so nothing links it to her. More importantly, her profile shows no wallet, so there’s nothing to watch in the first place.

    Why not deposit from her main wallet?

    That one public deposit would link her identity to the pool, and anyone could watch for her later withdrawals. A fresh, exchange-funded wallet avoids that link.

    Does SyndicateCopy ever hold her money?

    No. Only Priya’s keys can spend her notes in the pool, followers trade from their own wallets, and Flash executes orders without taking custody.

  2. Sign-up & Subscription

    Priya and Arjun verify; Arjun pays subscription & writes to Arkiv

    World IDProves each is a unique human without revealing who, blocks second accounts, and anchors Priya’s profile key.
    ArkivStores 30-day auto-expiring membership entity containing Arjun's public key & payment proof.

    Both scan World ID. Priya’s app creates her profile key PK_priya and binds it to her World ID. Arjun takes one seat, pays the monthly 20 USDC subscription directly to Priya's wallet on-chain, capturing paymentTxHash.

    Arjun’s phone receives/generates his encryption keypair (PUB_ENC_arjun / PRIV_ENC_arjun) and publishes a `Membership` entity directly to Arkiv set to auto-expire in 30 days:

    createEntity({
  schema: MembershipSchema,
  attributes: { leader: PK_priya, follower: account.address },
  payload: { pubKey: PUB_ENC_arjun, txHash: paymentTxHash },
  expiresIn: ExpirationTime.fromDays(30)
})

    Public sees: a paid transaction on-chain, and an entity on Arkiv indexed under Priya's leader address that automatically expires off state in 30 days. No names or private keys.

    Questions at this step
    What stops Priya from opening a second account?

    World ID gives the same ID for the same person in this app. A second sign-up shows the same ID and is rejected, so nobody can fake a record with many accounts.

    How does Arjun pay and prove his membership?

    He sends native tokens or USDC to the leader wallet, getting a `txHash`. His frontend then writes a `Membership` entity to Arkiv containing his public encryption key and `txHash`. Arkiv's built-in 30-day block expiration automatically revokes his active status when the month ends.

    What if someone writes a fake membership entity to Arkiv?

    Priya's client checks that the `paymentTxHash` in the Arkiv entity corresponds to an actual verified transaction to her wallet on-chain before encrypting signal keys for that user.

    How does Arjun get the room key safely?

    The key is encrypted to his device’s own public key before it’s sent, so only his phone can open it. It’s never posted anywhere readable.

    What happens when a follower leaves or expires?

    When a membership expires after 30 days, Arkiv automatically drops the entity. On the next broadcast, Priya queries active members, excluding expired followers from the key map.

    What if Priya loses her phone?

    Her profile key and pool keys must be backed up, like a wallet’s recovery phrase. Her World ID lets her bind a new profile key to the same identity, so her followers and record stay with her.

  3. Day 5, 10:00:00

    Priya posts a sealed promise

    ArkivStores the signed promise with a timestamp and no expiry, so her record can be checked later.

    Her app posts a fingerprint to Arkiv, signed with her profile key: “trade #17 will use a wallet I’m not naming yet.” It’s timestamped, so she can’t invent it after the fact.

    commitment = hash(0xB2B2…E002, trade #17, secret salt)

    Public sees: a random-looking hash.

    Questions at this step
    How does the system know the burner is Priya’s?

    No contract needs to know during the trade. This sealed promise, signed with her profile key, commits to the burner in advance. After the trade, she reveals it and anyone can check.

    Isn’t that link centralized on your server?

    No. Her profile key is bound to her World ID, the promise is timestamped on Arkiv, and anyone can verify the reveal. Our server never needs to hold the link.

    Can someone work out the burner from the fingerprint?

    No. The secret salt makes the hash impossible to reverse or guess, even by trying every new wallet on the chain.

    Could she claim someone else’s good trade?

    No. She’d have to have committed to that wallet before it traded, and the reveal requires a fingerprint that matches.

  4. Day 5, 10:00:05

    Money moves to a burner, without her account

    EF KohakuBuilds the zero-knowledge proof on her phone and sends it through a relayer, so no account of hers appears.

    Her phone builds a private proof: “I own enough notes in this pool.” A relayer sends it to the pool contract and pays the gas. The pool sends 6,050 USDC to the new burner 0xB2B2…E002, plus 3,940 USDC change back to Priya as a new private note.

    Public sees: the relayer called the pool, and the pool paid some new wallet. It could be any user.

    Questions at this step
    Isn’t this withdrawal sent from Priya’s account?

    No account of hers sends it. Her private pool address is just keys on her phone, not an on-chain account. The relayer submits the proof, and the contract checks the proof, not the sender.

    How does the pool know she has enough money?

    Her balance is a set of private “notes,” like sealed envelopes. The proof shows the notes exist, she owns them, they haven’t been spent, and inputs equal outputs (10,000 = 6,050 + 10 fee + 3,940 change). Without enough, no valid proof can be made.

    Where does the burner get gas money?

    Through the relayer, or withdrawn from the pool too. Never from a wallet linked to Priya: one such transfer would connect everything.

    Can the relayer see who she is?

    It sees only a proof. It could see the internet address the request came from, so the app routes requests through privacy networks rather than directly.

  5. Day 5, 10:00:10

    The burner buys ETH through Flash

    Definitive FlashPlaces one Bracket order (buy, stop-loss, take-profit), finds the best price across many sources, and lands it onchain.

    The burner signs one order: 2 ETH at about $3,000, with a stop-loss at $2,850 and a take-profit at $3,300. Our server forwards it, so Definitive sees the platform, not Priya’s internet address.

    Public sees: a brand-new wallet bought 2 ETH.

    Questions at this step
    Can’t people just watch the blockchain?

    They can see that someone bought ETH, but the wallet has no history and no link to Priya. Trades are anonymous, not invisible.

    Does Definitive learn it’s Priya?

    No. It sees an order signed by a new wallet, sent from our server.

    Why use Flash instead of a plain swap?

    One order carries the buy, stop-loss and take-profit, Flash finds the best price across many sources, and it lets us attach a fee to every copied trade from day one.

  6. Day 5, about 10:00:40

    Leader fetches active followers, encrypts signal & posts to Arkiv

    ArkivHolds encrypted envelope payload under a rotating HMAC tag with a 10-minute expiry.

    Priya's app queries Arkiv for all active non-expired memberships for PK_priya and retrieves public encryption keys (PUB_ENC_arjun, etc.).

    Her app generates a **fresh random symmetric key** (AES_K17) for this trade, encrypts the signal details with AES_K17, encrypts AES_K17 for each follower's public key, and signs with PK_priya. Our server posts the blob to Arkiv from 0xPOST…0007 under rotating tag `HMAC(roomKey, "signal" + 17)` set to expire in 10 minutes (`expiresIn: 600`).

    payload = {
  encryptedSignal: AES_Encrypt("Buy ETH @ $3,000, Stop $2,850, TP $3,300", AES_K17),
  keys: { "0xC3C3…A004": Encrypt_RSA(AES_K17, PUB_ENC_arjun) },
  signature: Sign(payload, PK_priya)
}

    Public sees: the platform posted an encrypted entry under a random-looking tag. Outsiders cannot read the signal or key mapping.

    Questions at this step
    If Priya keeps her trade private, what does the signal contain?

    What to trade: buy ETH around $3,000, stop $2,850, target $3,300. Not which wallet did it. Followers need only the first to copy from their own wallets.

    How does Priya find who to encrypt for?

    Her app queries Arkiv for active `Membership` entities tagged with her leader ID. Arkiv returns active followers and their public keys. She encrypts the signal once with a fresh AES key, and encrypts that AES key per active follower's public key.

    Why use a new AES key for every message?

    Per-message key generation ensures perfect forward secrecy and effortless revocation: when a follower's membership expires, they are omitted from the key mapping on future signals.

    Who posts it to Arkiv, and how?

    Priya’s app signs, encrypts and pads it. Our server writes it from one shared wallet used for every room. If our server is down or refuses, her app can post directly.

    createEntity({ payload: cipher, attributes: { tag, type: "signal" }, expiresIn: 600 })
    Anyone can download the encrypted signal. Is that safe?
    • Yes, as long as only followers hold valid decryption keys for the per-message AES key.
    • The key mapping changes on every broadcast.
    • We use a standard authenticated cipher (AES-GCM) from a proven library, never a homemade scheme.
    What can outsiders still learn without the key?

    That an entry was posted, and when. Rotating tags, fixed-size padding, a random delay and occasional decoy signals make that close to useless.

    Could someone post a fake signal as Priya?

    No. Followers’ apps check her profile-key signature and reject anything that doesn’t verify, even if our server is hacked.

    Why not use a new posting wallet every time?

    Each new wallet needs gas, and a common funder would link them all. Rotating tags plus one shared posting wallet hide far more, with no gas puzzle.

  7. Day 5, 10:01

    Arjun fetches, decrypts & executes (Default or Private Mode)

    ArkivHis app queries the tag it computed and reads the signal.
    Definitive FlashExecutes his copy as a TWAP split and attaches the 0.3% fee.
    EF KohakuPrivate mode only: transfers from Railgun pool to fresh burner via ZK proof & relayer.

    A push says only “New signal in Priya’s ETH Swings.” His app computes tag #17, fetches the entry from Arkiv, decrypts AES_K17 using PRIV_ENC_arjun, decrypts the signal, and checks Priya’s signature. He taps Copy with $500 across his chosen path:

    Path A (Default mode): the app waits a random 0 to 90 seconds, then Flash splits his order into 3 smaller pieces over about 4 minutes (TWAP) from his wallet 0xC3C3…A004. He ends up with 0.1663 ETH at an average $3,006.

    Path B (Private mode - opt-in): his money sits in the private pool. The app uses the **EF Kohaku SDK** to build a ZK proof, withdraws $500 through a relayer to a fresh burner 0xD5D5…F008, and executes the Flash copy anonymously.

    Fee: 0.3% of $500 is $1.50. $1.00 goes to Priya’s private address and $0.50 to the platform.

    Questions at this step
    How does Arjun find out a signal is waiting?

    A push notification with no trade details, since pushes pass through Apple’s or Google’s servers. His app then fetches and decrypts the entry from Arkiv. While open, it can also check Arkiv directly.

    How does his app find the right entry if the tag keeps changing?

    It holds the room key, so it computes the next expected tags (#17, #18…) and asks Arkiv for exactly those.

    How does Arjun decrypt the signal payload?

    His app queries Arkiv for tag #17, finds his wallet entry in the `keys` map, decrypts the 32-byte AES key using his private key (`PRIV_ENC_arjun`), and unlocks the trade details with AES-GCM.

    How does Private Mode execution work for a follower?

    It mirrors the leader's flow: Arjun's phone uses the EF Kohaku SDK to generate a zero-knowledge proof of his private pool notes. A relayer submits the withdrawal to a single-use burner wallet (`0xD5D5…F008`), which executes the trade on Flash and vanishes.

    Why does he get a slightly worse price?

    Priya’s buy moved the price a little before he copied. This is slippage. We show it openly, and for big rooms we spread followers’ orders over a few minutes.

    Are followers’ trades protected too?

    Yes, at two levels. By default, copies are spread out with a random delay and split into pieces, so there’s no sudden burst that reveals a signal. In private mode, a follower’s trades come from untraceable burners, just like the leader’s.

    Can Ravi just watch Arjun’s wallet and copy him?

    In default mode he could see Arjun’s buy, but only minutes after Priya’s and spread out, by which time much of the move has happened. In private mode there’s no wallet of Arjun’s to watch. Followers who care about this should use private mode.

    Why is private mode opt-in rather than the default?

    Every private trade pays relayer fees, about $10 in this example. On a $500 copy that’s 2%, which eats most of a typical gain. For larger amounts the fee is small and the privacy is worth it.

    Doesn’t spreading his order out cost him?

    The price can drift either way during those few minutes, and for small orders the effect is tiny. In return, bots have a much harder time jumping ahead of a crowd of followers, which usually saves more than it costs.

    What if he reposts the signal publicly?

    We can’t stop it, but each follower gets slightly different details, so a leaked copy points back to who shared it. Even leaked, it never exposes Priya’s wallet.

    Can fees reach Priya without revealing her?

    Yes. The private pool lets anyone pay her private address directly, so her fee share never touches a public wallet.

  8. Day 5, about 10:11

    The signal disappears

    ArkivExpires the entity automatically; no cleanup job on our side.

    It drops out of Arkiv’s searchable data, so nobody can copy a stale signal at a much worse price.

    Questions at this step
    What if a follower was offline?

    They miss that trade, which protects them from buying at a stale price. Their app keeps a history of signals it already opened, and auto-copy mode can trade for them using limited permissions they grant.

    Is it really gone?

    It leaves Arkiv’s searchable data, but an encrypted copy probably stays in chain history. If a key leaked later, only old, finished trades would be exposed, and rotating keys limits how many any one key opens.

    Why 10 minutes?

    Long enough for followers to react, short enough that late copies aren’t chasing a price that has already moved. Rooms could set their own window.

  9. Day 7

    Take-profit hits, and the burner is retired

    Definitive FlashTriggers the take-profit sell on its own, with nobody online.
    EF KohakuShields the proceeds back into the pool as a new private note for Priya.

    ETH reaches $3,300 and both orders sell automatically. The burner sends its 6,650 USDC back into the pool as a new private note for Priya, and is never used again.

    Public sees: the wallet sold ETH and deposited into the pool.

    Questions at this step
    Who triggers the sell if nobody is online?

    Flash holds the stop-loss and take-profit orders and executes them when the price is reached.

    Why never reuse the burner?

    Reuse lets anyone build a history for that wallet and spot it next time. One wallet per trade keeps every trade unlinked.

    How does her balance update?

    The deposit creates a new private note addressed to her. Her app finds it on the next scan, and her total rises to about 10,593 USDC.

  10. Day 7, after the trade closes

    Priya opens her sealed promise

    ArkivStores the reveal next to the promise, so anyone can query both and verify her record.

    She reveals that trade #17 used 0xB2B2…E002, with the salt. Anyone can check the fingerprint matches, that it was posted before the trade, and what the wallet earned.

    Public sees: one verified, finished trade on her record.

    Questions at this step
    Doesn’t revealing the burner expose her?

    Not usefully: the trade is over and the wallet is retired. Careless patterns across many reveals, like exact amounts or quick withdrawals, could hint at her funding, so the app varies amounts and timing.

    What stops her from hiding losing trades?

    Promises are numbered, so gaps are visible, and any promise not opened within 3 days counts as a maximum loss. Hiding a loss looks worse than showing it.

    How can I check her record myself?

    Recompute the fingerprint from the revealed wallet and salt, compare it with the timestamped promise on Arkiv, then look up that wallet’s trades on the chain.

    Could she prove her record without revealing wallets at all?

    Yes, with zero-knowledge proofs over her trades, but that’s research-level work. It’s on our roadmap, not in the hackathon build.

Bigger-picture questions

From judges
Why not just a paid Telegram group?

Telegram can’t prove a trader is one real person, can’t prove their record wasn’t edited, and doesn’t hide their wallet from copycats. It also sees every message.

What if your server goes down or lies?

Signals and promises live on Arkiv, signed by Priya’s key. Her app can post directly, and anyone can verify her record without us.

Isn’t this just hiding money?

It protects a trader’s strategy from copycats and front-running bots. Every person is a verified human, and Privacy Pools, coming to Kohaku, lets users prove their funds aren’t from known illicit sources.

About the business
Is selling trading signals legal?

It can count as investment advice; in India, SEBI regulates this. A real launch needs licensed leaders or a structure reviewed by lawyers. The hackathon build runs on test funds.

How do you get the first traders?

Leaders earn from day one through fees on every copied trade, and a checkable record helps good but unknown traders stand out against fake leaderboards.

How does SyndicateCopy make money?

A share of the fee on every copied trade, attached through Flash. In the example, $0.50 of each $1.50.

What we hide, and what we don’t

Hidden

  • The signal’s contents, from anyone without an active membership key
  • Which signals belong to which room, thanks to rotating HMAC tags
  • That a trade is Priya’s, until she reveals it after it closes
  • Priya’s and private-mode followers' balances, which exist only as private notes

Weakened, not hidden

  • That the platform posted something, and roughly when
  • Timing matches between a trade and a post, reduced by random delays and decoys
  • Bursts of followers buying together, blurred by random delays and split orders
  • Patterns in revealed past trades, reduced by varying amounts and timing

Still public

  • That someone bought ETH: trades are anonymous, not invisible
  • Default-mode followers’ trades from their own wallets (private mode hides them)
  • Leaked signals: we can trace leakers, not stop them
